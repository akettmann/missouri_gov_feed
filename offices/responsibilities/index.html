What each office does

Legal text checked 2026-10-27. Written from docs/reference/office_responsibilities.md.

Legal text checked 2026-10-27. Each bullet is one responsibility, written in plain words, with a link to the law that creates it. This describes the job, not how any officeholder does it. The layout is the same for every office.

How to read it:

  • Duty means the law says the office must do it. Power means the law allows it. Where the law does not make that split clear, the bullet says "Job".
  • "Data" names the project source that covers the responsibility, or says "no data yet". "Not loaded yet" marks a source we have researched but not loaded.
  • Numbered notes are at the end of each office. They hold exceptions and conditions.
  • The Missouri Constitution and the Revised Statutes of Missouri (RSMo) are quoted from revisor.mo.gov. The U.S. Constitution is quoted from the National Archives transcript. Quotes are short so the exact words can be checked. The law changes. Check the linked page for the current text.
  • Words used: a bill is a proposed law. An appropriation is a law that lets the state spend money for a stated purpose. A veto is the Governor's refusal to sign a bill. Advice and consent means the Senate must approve an appointment. Impeachment is the formal charge that starts removal of an official from office.

  • DutyPass bills that cover one subject. "No bill shall contain more than one subject which shall be clearly expressed in its title", except general appropriation bills and one other exception in the section. (Mo. Const. art. III, §23)Data: bill text (Missouri House bill documents)

Notes

  1. The Speaker of the House is in the line of succession to be Governor. See the Governor notes. (Mo. Const. art. IV, §11(a))
  2. If the Governor returns any bill with objections on or after the fifth day before the last day of the session, the legislature meets again automatically in September, for up to ten days, only to consider vetoed bills. (Mo. Const. art. III, §32) Bills vetoed after the legislature adjourns go to the Secretary of State. (Mo. Const. art. III, §31)

  • DutySee that the laws are carried out. "The governor shall take care that the laws are distributed and faithfully executed." (Mo. Const. art. IV, §2)Data: no data yet
  • DutyAct on every bill within the time limit. Within 15 days of getting a bill, the Governor returns it signed or with objections. After the legislature adjourns, the limit is 45 days. A bill not returned in time becomes law. (Mo. Const. art. III, §31)Data: bill actions (Missouri House, Missouri Senate bill tracking)
  • DutySend the legislature a budget plan within 30 days after a regular session starts. (Mo. Const. art. IV, §24)Data: no data yet
  • DutyReport on the state of the government. The Governor gives the legislature this information "at the commencement of each session". (Mo. Const. art. IV, §9)Data: no data yet
  • PowerVeto a bill. A veto can be overridden by two-thirds of each house. (Mo. Const. art. III, §32)Data: bill actions (Missouri House, Missouri Senate bill tracking)
  • PowerVeto single items in a spending bill. The Governor "may object to one or more items or portions of items of appropriation of money". Money for public schools and for paying public debt cannot be cut this way. (Mo. Const. art. IV, §26)Data: bill actions; no data yet on item vetoes
  • PowerSlow or cut spending when revenue falls short. The Governor "may control the rate at which any appropriation is expended" and may cut spending when "actual revenues are less than the revenue estimates". (Mo. Const. art. IV, §27)Data: what agencies paid, by fiscal year, with partial coverage (data.mo.gov State Expenditures)
  • PowerCall a special session. "On extraordinary occasions he may convene the general assembly by proclamation." (Mo. Const. art. IV, §9)Data: bill actions in special sessions (Missouri Senate bill tracking)
  • PowerAppoint. The Governor fills vacancies in public offices "unless otherwise provided by law", and appoints department heads with Senate approval. (Mo. Const. art. IV, §4, art. IV, §17)Data: no data yet
  • PowerAppoint judges to open seats on the courts that use the Missouri nonpartisan court plan, from a list of three names. (Mo. Const. art. V, §25(a))Data: judge careers (Missouri Official Manual, CourtListener judge records)
  • PowerGrant pardons, commutations (shorter sentences) and reprieves (delays) "after conviction, for all offenses except treason and cases of impeachment". (Mo. Const. art. IV, §7)Data: no data yet
  • PowerCommand the state militia (the National Guard). The Governor "shall be the commander in chief of the militia, except when it is called into the service of the United States." (Mo. Const. art. IV, §6)Data: no data yet

Notes

  1. If the Governor dies, resigns, or is convicted or impeached, the Lieutenant Governor becomes Governor for the rest of the term. If the Governor is absent or cannot serve, the Lieutenant Governor takes over the powers until the problem ends. (Mo. Const. art. IV, §11(a))
  2. A Governor can be impeached by the House and tried by a special commission picked by the Senate. (Mo. Const. art. VII, §1, art. VII, §2)
  3. Terms are four years. No one may be elected Governor more than twice. (Mo. Const. art. IV, §17)

Notes

  1. Terms: 12 years for Supreme Court and Court of Appeals judges, 6 years for circuit judges, 4 years for associate circuit judges. (Mo. Const. art. V, §19) Data: Missouri Official Manual, Missouri Secretary of State official returns (judicial retention results) for retention votes.
  2. Seats on the Supreme Court, the Court of Appeals, and the circuit courts of St. Louis city and Jackson County are filled by Governor appointment from a three-name list from a nonpartisan commission. (Mo. Const. art. (Mo. Const. art. V, §25(b))
  3. Each circuit's judges pick a presiding judge, who has "general administrative authority over the court". (Mo. Const. art. V, §15)
  4. Judges can be impeached for "crimes, misconduct, habitual drunkenness, willful neglect of duty, corruption in office, incompetency, or any offense involving moral turpitude or oppression in office." (Mo. Const. art. VII, §1) An impeached Supreme Court judge is tried by a special commission of seven jurists chosen by the Senate; other impeached judges are tried before the Supreme Court. (Mo. Const. art. VII, §2)

  • DutyDecide the cases the Constitution assigns to federal courts. The power "shall extend to all Cases, in Law and Equity, arising under this Constitution, the Laws of the United States, and Treaties". It also covers cases involving ambassadors, maritime law, the United States as a party, and disputes between states. (U.S. Const. art. III, §2)Data: dockets (CourtListener court records, CourtListener API)
  • JobServe for life, if they keep up good behavior. Judges "shall hold their Offices during good Behaviour". Their pay cannot be cut while they hold office. (U.S. Const. art. III, §1)Data: Federal Judicial Center

Notes

  1. The President appoints federal judges with the Senate's advice and consent. (U.S. Const. art. II, §2)
  2. Federal judges can be impeached by the House and tried by the Senate. (U.S. Const. art. I, §2 and §3)

Offices not covered yet

Sheriffs, prosecutors and other local officers, county and city officials, and the State Auditor's local audits beyond the Constitution's wording are not in this first version. Statewide boards and commissions an office sits on by law (for example, the Governor and Auditor approving state bank deposits, noted above) are listed only where the Constitution says so. Statutes that add duties are listed only for the Attorney General.
