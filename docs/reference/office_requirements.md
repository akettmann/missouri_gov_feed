# Legal requirements to hold or run for each office

Legal text checked 2026-10-27 (issue #296). Each bullet is one requirement, written in plain words, with a link to the
law that sets it. Reading level: Flesch-Kincaid grade about 7 with the quoted legal wording (7.1) and about 6 without it (5.7), measured
with the Python `textstat` package on the page text with abbreviations protected. That is under the grade-12 target. This describes what the law says a
person needs to hold or run for the office. It does not say whether any officeholder or candidate meets the rules. The
layout is the same for every office, and matches `docs/reference/office_responsibilities.md` (what each office does).

How to read it:

- Each office lists the same items, in this order: **Age**, **Citizenship**, **Residency**, **Voter**, **Other
  qualifications**, **Terms**, **Term limit**, **How filled** (election or appointment) and **Filing**. A bullet that
  says "No rule found" means the sources named for that office were read and do not set that requirement. It does not
  prove no other law does.
- Notes marked `[n]` are at the end of each office. They hold exceptions and conditions.
- The Missouri Constitution and the Revised Statutes of Missouri (RSMo) are quoted from revisor.mo.gov. The U.S.
  Constitution and its amendments are quoted from the National Archives transcripts. Quotes are short so the exact
  words can be checked. Laws change by amendment or new statute. Check the linked page for the current text.
- Words used: a **qualified voter** is a person who meets the voting rules in Missouri Constitution art. VIII, §2:
  a U.S. citizen over 18 who lives in Missouri and the local area and is registered within the time the law sets.
  The same section excludes anyone with a court-appointed guardian "by reason of mental incapacity", anyone
  "involuntarily confined in a mental institution" by a court, and says persons convicted of a felony "may be excluded
  by law" ([art. VIII, §2][mc-viii-2]). An
  **inhabitant** is a person who lives in the place. A **declaration of candidacy** is the sworn form a person files to
  get on the ballot. A **felony** is a crime that the felony law cited in each rule counts as a felony. A **retention
  election** is a yes-or-no vote on whether a judge keeps the job.

## Rules for every Missouri candidate

These filing rules apply to people who run for the Missouri offices below and, as written, to "elective public office in
the state of Missouri". Each office section links back here.

- **Felony.** "No person shall qualify as a candidate for elective public office in the state of Missouri who has been
  found guilty of or pled guilty to a felony under the federal laws of the United States of America or to a felony under
  the laws of this state or an offense committed in another state that would be considered a felony in this state."
  ([RSMo 115.306][rsmo-115-306])
- **Taxes.** A candidate is out of the race if "delinquent in the payment of any state income taxes, personal property
  taxes, municipal taxes, real property taxes on the place of residence", or if "a past or present corporate officer of
  any fee office that owes any taxes to the state". A candidate must file a sworn tax statement
  with the Department of Revenue and attach a copy to the declaration of candidacy.
  ([RSMo 115.306][rsmo-115-306])
- **Filing window.** File the declaration of candidacy "by 5:00 p.m. on the last Tuesday in March immediately preceding
  the primary election". It cannot be accepted before "8:00 a.m. on the last Tuesday in February".
  ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349])
- **Sworn form.** The declaration says the candidate is "a resident and registered voter of the county ... and the state
  of Missouri". ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349])
- **In person.** "Each declaration of candidacy for nomination in a primary election shall be filed by the candidate in
  person". ([RSMo 115.355][rsmo-115-355])
- **Filing fee.** Paid to the party's committee before filing: $500 for a statewide office or U.S. senator, $300 for a
  U.S. representative, circuit judge or state senator, and $150 for a state representative.
  ([RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])

Notes:

- [1] The felony rule has a court ruling on pardons: a governor's pardon "extinguishes the fact of a felony
  conviction, but does not erase the fact of a felony guilty plea." (Noted on [RSMo 115.306][rsmo-115-306]: Fletcher v.
  Young, 689 S.W.3d 161 (Mo. banc 2024).) The section's history ends "A.L. 2026 H.B. 1871" with no future-effective marker on revisor.mo.gov, so the text
  quoted here (as shown on 2026-10-27) is very likely the version in effect since 2026-08-28. Re-check the wording
  before relying on it.
- [2] The tax rule has a repair path. If the Department of Revenue finds a false tax statement, the candidate gets
  "thirty days to remit" the tax owed that is not in dispute. If the candidate does not pay, the candidate is
  disqualified "and barred from refiling for an entire election cycle". ([RSMo 115.306][rsmo-115-306])
- [3] A candidate may file by certified mail if unable to appear because of physical disability (with a doctor's
  sworn statement) or on active duty in the U.S. Armed Forces (with a commanding officer's sworn statement). A judge
  seeking retention may also file by certified mail. ([RSMo 115.355][rsmo-115-355])
- [4] A candidate who cannot pay the fee may file a "declaration of inability to pay" with a petition of signatures: at
  least one-half of one percent of the votes cast for the office at the last election for a statewide office, or one
  percent for other offices. "No filing fee shall be required of any person who proposes to be an independent
  candidate, the candidate of a new party or a candidate for presidential elector."
  ([RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])
- [5] The Missouri Constitution adds a general rule for any civil office: no person shall be elected or appointed "who is
  not a citizen of the United States, and who shall not have resided in this state one year next preceding his election
  or appointment", except that residence "shall not be necessary in cases of appointment to administrative positions
  requiring technical or specialized skill or knowledge." ([Mo. Const. art. VII, §8][mc-vii-8])
- [6] The filing window and fee rules are for candidates who run in a primary on a party ticket. Rules for independent
  and new-party candidates are in RSMo chapter 115 and are not covered here.

## Missouri House of Representatives

- **Age.** At least 24 years old. "Each representative shall be twenty-four years of age".
  ([Mo. Const. art. III, §4][mc-iii-4])
- **Citizenship.** U.S. citizen. See the general rule in note [5] of "Rules for every Missouri candidate".
  ([Mo. Const. art. VII, §8][mc-vii-8])
- **Residency.** Live in the county or district for one year before the election. A representative must "next before the day of his
  election" have been "a resident of the county or district which he is chosen to represent for one year".
  ([Mo. Const. art. III, §4][mc-iii-4])
- **Voter.** Be a qualified voter for two years before the election. ([Mo. Const. art. III, §4][mc-iii-4])
- **Other qualifications.** Not guilty of or pleaded guilty to a felony; no unpaid taxes of the kinds listed in RSMo 115.306 (see "Rules for every Missouri candidate").
  ([RSMo 115.306][rsmo-115-306])
- **Terms.** Two years: the House has "one hundred sixty-three members elected at each general election", and general
  elections are held every two years.
  ([Mo. Const. art. III, §3][mc-iii-3])
- **Term limit.** "No one shall be elected to serve more than eight years total in any one house of the General Assembly
  nor more than sixteen years total in both houses". ([Mo. Const. art. III, §8][mc-iii-8])
- **How filled.** Elected at each general election. ([Mo. Const. art. III, §3][mc-iii-3])
- **Filing.** Declaration of candidacy and a $150 fee. See "Rules for every Missouri candidate".
  ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349], [RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])

Notes:

- [1] Term-limit counting. Service from an election before December 3, 1992 "shall not be counted". Neither is "service
  of less than one year, in the case of a member of the house of representatives, or two years, in the case of a member
  of the senate, by a person elected after the effective date of this section to complete the term of another person".
  ([Mo. Const. art. III, §8][mc-iii-8])
- [2] "Resident" does not mean physical presence for every day of the period. It means "that place where a man has his
  true fixed and permanent home and principal establishment and to which whenever he is absent he has the intention of
  returning." (Noted on [Mo. Const. art. IV, §3][mc-iv-3]: State ex rel. King v. Walsh, 484 S.W.2d 641 (Mo. 1972),
  about the Governor's ten-year rule.)
- [3] Courts have upheld the age rule, with age counted from "date of birth, rather than his date of conception"
  (Stiles v. Blunt, 912 F.2d 260 (8th Cir. 1990)), and the two-year voter rule (Peters v. Johns, 489 S.W.3d 262 (Mo.
  2016)). (Both noted on [Mo. Const. art. III, §4][mc-iii-4].)
- [4] If a new district is drawn, the one-year rule is met by living in the "county or district from which the same shall
  have been taken". ([Mo. Const. art. III, §4][mc-iii-4])

## Missouri Senate

- **Age.** At least 30 years old. "Each senator shall be thirty years of age".
  ([Mo. Const. art. III, §6][mc-iii-6])
- **Citizenship.** U.S. citizen. See the general rule in note [5] of "Rules for every Missouri candidate".
  ([Mo. Const. art. VII, §8][mc-vii-8])
- **Residency.** Live in the district for one year before the election: "a resident of the district which he is chosen to
  represent for one year". ([Mo. Const. art. III, §6][mc-iii-6])
- **Voter.** Be a "qualified voter of the state for three years" before the election.
  ([Mo. Const. art. III, §6][mc-iii-6])
- **Other qualifications.** Not guilty of or pleaded guilty to a felony; no unpaid taxes of the kinds listed in RSMo 115.306 (see "Rules for every Missouri candidate").
  ([RSMo 115.306][rsmo-115-306])
- **Terms.** "The senate shall consist of thirty-four members elected by the qualified voters of the senatorial
  districts for a term of four years." ([Mo. Const. art. III, §5][mc-iii-5])
- **Term limit.** Eight years in the Senate and sixteen in both houses together.
  ([Mo. Const. art. III, §8][mc-iii-8])
- **How filled.** Elected by the voters of the district. ([Mo. Const. art. III, §5][mc-iii-5])
- **Filing.** Declaration of candidacy and a $300 fee. See "Rules for every Missouri candidate".
  ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349], [RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])

Notes:

- [1] Term-limit counting. The same exceptions apply as for the House. Service of less than two years in the Senate to
  finish another person's term does not count. ([Mo. Const. art. III, §8][mc-iii-8])
- [2] A court has held that the one-year district residency rule does not violate the equal protection clause of the
  Fourteenth Amendment. (Noted on [Mo. Const. art. III, §6][mc-iii-6]: State ex rel. Gralike v. Walsh, 483 S.W.2d 70
  (Mo. 1972).)
- [3] If a new district is drawn, the one-year rule is met by living in the "district or districts from which the same
  shall have been taken". ([Mo. Const. art. III, §6][mc-iii-6])

## Governor

- **Age.** "At least thirty years old". ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §3][mc-iv-3])
- **Citizenship.** "A citizen of the United States for at least fifteen years". ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §3][mc-iv-3])
- **Residency.** "A resident of this state at least ten years next before election".
  ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §3][mc-iv-3])
- **Voter.** No separate voter rule found in art. IV, §3.
- **Other qualifications.** Not guilty of or pleaded guilty to a felony; no unpaid taxes of the kinds listed in RSMo 115.306 (see "Rules for every Missouri candidate").
  ([RSMo 115.306][rsmo-115-306])
- **Terms.** Four years. The Governor is "elected at the presidential elections for terms of four years".
  ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §17][mc-iv-17])
- **Term limit.** "No person shall be elected governor or treasurer more than twice". ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §17][mc-iv-17])
- **How filled.** Elected at the presidential elections. The Lieutenant Governor becomes Governor on the Governor's death, conviction, impeachment or resignation.
  ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §17][mc-iv-17], [art. IV, §11(a)][mc-iv-11a])
- **Filing.** Declaration of candidacy and a $500 fee. See "Rules for every Missouri candidate".
  ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349], [RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])

Notes:

- [1] Term-limit counting. A person who held or acted as Governor "for more than two years of a term to which some
  other person was elected" can be elected only once more. ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §17][mc-iv-17])
- [2] "Resident" does not mean ten years of unbroken presence. It means the person's "true fixed and permanent home".
  (Noted on [Mo. Const. art. IV, §3][mc-iv-3]: State ex rel. King v. Walsh, 484 S.W.2d 641 (Mo. 1972).)

  ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §3][mc-iv-3])

## Attorney General

- **Age.** No rule found in art. IV, §12 or §17 of the Constitution or in RSMo 27.010.
- **Citizenship.** U.S. citizen. See the general rule in note [5] of "Rules for every Missouri candidate".

  ([Mo. Const. art. IV, §17][mc-iv-17])

  ([RSMo 27.010][rsmo-27-010], [Mo. Const. art. IV, §17][mc-iv-17])
- **Filing.** Declaration of candidacy and a $500 fee. See "Rules for every Missouri candidate".
  ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349], [RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])

Notes:

- [1] Many people expect the Attorney General to be a licensed lawyer. The sections read for this page do not say so.

## Missouri judges (Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, circuit courts)

- **Age.** Supreme Court and Court of Appeals judges "shall be at least thirty years of age". Circuit judges must be "not
  less than thirty years of age". Associate circuit judges must be "at least twenty-five years old".
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §21][mc-v-21])
- **Citizenship.** Supreme Court and Court of Appeals judges: U.S. citizens "for at least fifteen years". Circuit judges:
  "at least ten years". Associate circuit judges: no citizenship years listed; the general rule in note [5] of "Rules for
  every Missouri candidate" applies. ([Mo. Const. art. V, §21][mc-v-21], [art. VII, §8][mc-vii-8])
- **Residency.** Court of Appeals judges must live in their district. Circuit judges must be "residents of the circuit
  for at least one year". Associate circuit judges must be "residents of the county". Supreme Court judges: no
  district rule found. ([Mo. Const. art. V, §21][mc-v-21])
- **Voter.** Supreme Court and Court of Appeals judges: "qualified voters of the state for nine years". Circuit judges:
  "three years". Associate circuit judges: "qualified voters of this state". ([Mo. Const. art. V, §21][mc-v-21])
- **Other qualifications.** "Every supreme, appellate, circuit, and associate circuit court judge shall be licensed to
  practice law in this state." ([Mo. Const. art. V, §21][mc-v-21]) Judges "shall retire at the age of seventy years".
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §26][mc-v-26])
- **Terms.** "Twelve years" for the Supreme Court and Court of Appeals, "six years" for circuit judges and "four years"
  for associate circuit judges. A judge chosen under the court plan serves a shorter first term: see note [8].
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §19][mc-v-19])
- **Term limit.** No rule found.
- **How filled.** By the nonpartisan court plan in some courts and by election in others, depending on the court and, for
  some circuits, a local vote. See notes [1] and [2]. ([Mo. Const. art. V, §25(a)][mc-v-25a],
  [art. V, §25(b)][mc-v-25b])
- **Filing.** Judges chosen by election file like other candidates: see "Rules for every Missouri candidate", with the
  fee for circuit judge ($300); RSMo 115.357 lists no separate fee for associate circuit judges. Judges under the court plan file a declaration to run for retention. See note [3].
  ([RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357], [Mo. Const. art. V, §25(c)(1)][mc-v-25c1])

Notes:

- [1] Court plan. When a seat opens on the Supreme Court, the Court of Appeals, or the circuit or associate circuit
  courts in the city of St. Louis and Jackson County, "the governor shall fill such vacancy by appointing one of three
  persons ... nominated ... by a nonpartisan judicial commission". If the Governor does not choose within 60 days, the
  commission picks one of its nominees. ([Mo. Const. art. V, §25(a)][mc-v-25a])
- [2] Other circuits. Voters in a circuit outside St. Louis and Jackson County can vote to adopt the court plan or drop
  it.
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §25(b)][mc-v-25b]) Associate circuit judges are picked the same way as the circuit judge in
  their circuit; where the plan does not apply, they "shall be elected in the county in which they are to serve".
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §16][mc-v-16])
- [3] Retention. A judge on the court plan can file with the Secretary of State a declaration to run to succeed
  himself or herself, at least 60 days before the general election before the term ends. The voters then see a separate
  ballot "without party designation". If no declaration is filed, the seat is filled by appointment.
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §25(c)(1)][mc-v-25c1])
- [4] Retirement. A retired judge, "with his consent, may be assigned by the supreme court as a senior judge".
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §26][mc-v-26])
- [5] The age 70 retirement rule has been upheld against a federal age discrimination claim. (Noted on
  [Mo. Const. art. V, §26][mc-v-26]: Gregory v. Ashcroft, 111 S. Ct. 2395 (1991).)
- [6] Court of Appeals judges must live in their district "except as provided by section 6" of article V.
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §21][mc-v-21])
- [7] The citizenship and voter years for Supreme Court, Court of Appeals and circuit judges are counted back from
  their "selection" ("next preceding their selection"). ([Mo. Const. art. V, §21][mc-v-21])
- [8] First term. "Each judge appointed pursuant to the provisions of sections 25(a)-(g) shall hold office for a term
  ending December thirty-first following the next general election after the expiration of twelve months in the
  office." So an appointed judge serves until the retention vote, then the full term.
  ([Mo. Const. art. V, §25(c)(1)][mc-v-25c1])

## U.S. House of Representatives

- **Age.** "Attained to the Age of twenty five Years". ([U.S. Const. art. I, §2][us-const])
- **Citizenship.** "Seven Years a Citizen of the United States". ([U.S. Const. art. I, §2][us-const])
- **Residency.** "When elected, be an Inhabitant of that State in which he shall be chosen." The Constitution does not
  require living in the district. ([U.S. Const. art. I, §2][us-const])
- **Voter.** No rule found.
- **Other qualifications.** No one can hold another U.S. office and be a member: "no Person holding any Office under the
  United States, shall be a Member of either House during his Continuance in Office."
  ([U.S. Const. art. I, §6][us-const]) No one who previously took an oath, as an officer of the United States or of a State, to support the Constitution and
  then "engaged in insurrection or rebellion against the same, or given aid or comfort to the enemies thereof" can
  serve unless two-thirds of each house vote to remove the bar.
  ([U.S. Const. amend. XIV, §3][us-amend])
- **Terms.** "Chosen every second Year by the People of the several States." ([U.S. Const. art. I, §2][us-const])
- **Term limit.** No rule found in the Constitution's text.
- **How filled.** Elected. "The Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections for Senators and Representatives, shall be
  prescribed in each State by the Legislature thereof; but the Congress may at any time by Law make or alter such
  Regulations". ([U.S. Const. art. I, §4][us-const])
- **Filing.** In Missouri, a candidate for U.S. representative files a declaration of candidacy and pays $300. See "Rules
  for every Missouri candidate". ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349], [RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])

Notes:

- [1] Each house "shall be the Judge of the Elections, Returns and Qualifications of its own Members".
  ([U.S. Const. art. I, §5][us-const])
- [2] The Fourteenth Amendment bar can be lifted by "a vote of two-thirds of each House".
  ([U.S. Const. amend. XIV, §3][us-amend])
- [3] No religious test is allowed: "no religious Test shall ever be required as a Qualification to any Office or public
  Trust under the United States." ([U.S. Const. art. VI][us-const])
- [4] The Missouri rules in "Rules for every Missouri candidate" are state ballot-access rules written in terms of
  "elective public office in the state of Missouri". This page does not decide how they apply to a candidate for Congress.

## U.S. Senate

- **Age.** "Attained to the Age of thirty Years". ([U.S. Const. art. I, §3][us-const])
- **Citizenship.** "Nine Years a Citizen of the United States". ([U.S. Const. art. I, §3][us-const])
- **Residency.** "When elected, be an Inhabitant of that State for which he shall be chosen."
  ([U.S. Const. art. I, §3][us-const])
- **Voter.** No rule found.
- **Other qualifications.** Same as for the House: no other U.S. office while a member, and the Fourteenth Amendment bar.
  ([U.S. Const. art. I, §6][us-const], [amend. XIV, §3][us-amend])
- **Terms.** Six years. "Two Senators from each State, elected by the people thereof, for six years."
  ([U.S. Const. amend. XVII][us-amend])
- **Term limit.** No rule found in the Constitution's text.
- **How filled.** Elected by the people of the state. If a seat opens, "the executive authority of such State shall issue
  writs of election", and the legislature "may empower the executive thereof to make temporary appointments until the
  people fill the vacancies by election". ([U.S. Const. amend. XVII][us-amend])
- **Filing.** In Missouri, a candidate for U.S. senator files a declaration of candidacy and pays $500. See "Rules for
  every Missouri candidate". ([RSMo 115.349][rsmo-115-349], [RSMo 115.357][rsmo-115-357])

Notes:

- [1] The original wording said Senators were "chosen by the Legislature thereof". The 17th Amendment changed this to
  election by the people. ([U.S. Const. art. I, §3][us-const], [amend. XVII][us-amend])
- [2] The same notes apply as for the House on judging qualifications, religious tests, and Missouri ballot rules.

## Federal judges

- **Age.** No rule found in Article III or Article II, §2.
- **Citizenship.** No rule found in Article III or Article II, §2.
- **Residency.** No rule found in Article III or Article II, §2.
- **Voter.** No rule found.
- **Other qualifications.** No rule found in the Constitution's text. Judges swear an oath to support the Constitution:
  "all executive and judicial Officers, both of the United States and of the several States, shall be bound by Oath or
  Affirmation, to support this Constitution". ([U.S. Const. art. VI][us-const])
- **Terms.** For life, with good behavior. "The Judges, both of the supreme and inferior Courts, shall hold their Offices
  during good Behaviour". ([U.S. Const. art. III, §1][us-const])
- **Term limit.** No rule found.
- **How filled.** Appointed, not elected. The President "shall nominate, and by and with the Advice and Consent of the
  Senate, shall appoint ... Judges of the supreme Court, and all other Officers of the United States".
  ([U.S. Const. art. II, §2][us-const])
- **Filing.** None. There is no ballot filing for federal judges.

Notes:

- [1] Congress may add rules by statute. Statutes on federal judgeships are not covered in this version.
- [2] Federal judges can be impeached by the House and tried by the Senate.
  ([U.S. Const. art. I, §2 and §3][us-const])

## Offices not covered yet

Local offices (county, city, sheriff, prosecutor, school board), municipal judges, and Missouri statutes on special
elections, vacancies and write-in candidates are not in this version. Independent and new-party candidacy rules (RSMo
chapter 115) are linked above but not summarized. Federal statutes on judges (28 U.S.C.) are not covered.
