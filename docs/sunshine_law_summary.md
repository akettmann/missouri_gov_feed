# The Missouri Sunshine Law in brief

A plain-language summary of RSMo Chapter 610, the Sunshine Law. Written from the statute text saved on
2026-10-19 ([full text](reference/rsmo_chapter_610_sunshine_law.md)). It is a reading aid, not legal advice:
the statute and the courts' readings of it control. How we use it for our own requests is in
[sunshine_requests.md](sunshine_requests.md).

## The rule: open unless a law says otherwise

Meetings, records, votes, actions and deliberations of public governmental bodies are open to the public
unless a law provides otherwise. The law is to be read broadly, and its exceptions narrowly (§ 610.011).
Public records are presumed open (§ 610.022.5).

## Who it covers

"Public governmental body" (§ 610.010(4)) is broad:
- State and local legislative, administrative and governmental bodies: departments, boards, commissions,
  councils, school districts, special districts, and committees and advisory bodies they appoint.
- Public universities and colleges supported by state funds.
- Some private "quasi-public" bodies, mainly those whose main purpose is contracting with government, or
  that steer tax credits, public debt or eminent domain.
- Courts only "when operating in an administrative capacity". Court records are governed by Court
  Operating Rule 2 instead ([text](reference/court_operating_rule_2.md)).

"Public record" means any record a public body keeps, on paper or electronically. That includes reports a
paid consultant prepared for it, and records a private contractor keeps for it (§ 610.010(6)).

## Meetings

- **Notice:** time, date, place and a tentative agenda, at least 24 hours ahead, not counting weekends and
  holidays (§ 610.020). Shorter notice needs good cause, stated in the minutes.
- **Recording:** anyone may record an open meeting by audio or video. The body can set rules to limit
  disruption (§ 610.020.3).
- **Minutes and votes:** the body keeps minutes, and records every member's vote on each roll call
  (§§ 610.015, 610.020).

## What can be closed

Section 610.021 lists the only subjects that may be closed. Closing is allowed, never required
(§ 610.022.4). The list includes:
- legal actions and privileged communications with the body's lawyers
- real estate deals where publicity would affect the price
- hiring, firing, discipline or promotion of particular employees, and individually identifiable personnel
  records (but names, positions, salaries and lengths of service of public employees stay open)
- health, welfare and student records of identifiable people
- negotiation strategy, sealed bids before opening, and bid specifications before approval
- test materials before the test is given
- security systems, plans, access codes and network configurations
- records another law makes confidential
- a few narrow categories added over time: municipal abuse hotlines, auditors' work papers,
  endangered-species locations, minors' identifying information, utility and park-reservation customer
  data, and others

**How to close:** a meeting or vote is closed only by a public, recorded vote of a majority of a quorum.
The body must cite the specific exception, give notice of the closed meeting with its reason, and discuss
only that subject (§ 610.022). A member who thinks a closure is illegal can object on the record.

**Partly closed records:** when only part of a record is closed, the body separates the closed part and
releases the rest (§ 610.024).

Separate sections cover law enforcement records (§§ 610.100–610.200):
- Incident and arrest reports are open (§ 610.100).
- Investigative reports, mobile video and similar records are closed until the investigation becomes
  inactive. Parts that would endanger a person or an investigation can be withheld.
- When a charged case ends in dismissal, nolle prosequi, acquittal or a suspended imposition of sentence,
  its official records close (§ 610.105).

## Asking for records

- **Who to ask:** each body appoints a custodian of records, and must tell you who and where that is
  (§ 610.023.1).
- **How:** the law requires no particular form, and you don't have to say why you want the records. A
  written request (email or letter) gives you a clear start date. Our templates are in
  [sunshine_requests.md](sunshine_requests.md).
- **Deadline:** the body must act "as soon as possible, but in no event later than the end of the third
  business day" after it receives the request. If it can't produce the records by then, it must explain
  the delay and give the earliest date they will be available (§ 610.023.3).
- **Format:** if you ask for a format the body has, it must use it (§ 610.023.3). Electronic records are
  covered too (§§ 610.025, 610.029).
- **Denials:** on request, the custodian must give the reason in writing, citing the specific law, by the
  end of the third business day after you ask for that statement (§ 610.023.4).

## Fees

From § 610.026:
- At most 10 cents a page for paper copies up to 9 × 14 inches. Duplicating time is charged at no more
  than the average clerical hourly rate.
- Research time can be charged at actual cost. Attorney review time can't (*Gross v. Parson*, 2021).
- For electronic records: the copies, the medium and staff time. Programming beyond what the request
  normally needs can be charged at actual cost.
- You can ask for an estimate first. The body can require payment in advance. A request is treated as
  withdrawn if fees aren't paid within 90 days (150 days for bills over $1,000).
- Fees can be waived or reduced when the release is in the public interest, meaning it is likely to
  "contribute significantly to public understanding" of the body's operations and is not primarily
  commercial.

## Remedies

From § 610.027:
- **Who can sue:** any aggrieved person, Missouri taxpayer or citizen, the Attorney General or a
  prosecuting attorney, in the circuit court of the county where the body is based.
- **Burden of proof:** once you show the body is covered and closed a meeting, record or vote, the body has
  to prove it complied.
- **Penalties:** a knowing violation carries a civil penalty of up to $1,000, and the court may award
  costs and attorney fees. A purposeful violation carries up to $5,000, and the court must award costs and
  fees.
- **Voiding actions:** a court can void an action taken in violation, if the public interest in
  enforcement outweighs keeping the action.
- **Time limit:** sue within one year of when the violation could be discovered, and never more than two
  years after it happened.
- **Body in doubt:** a body unsure whether it may close something can ask a court, its attorney, or the
  Attorney General for an opinion.

Short of a lawsuit, the Attorney General's office takes Sunshine complaints and mediates them.
