# House rules and landmark procedural events, 114th-119th Congress

Every Congress opens by adopting a rules package, usually `H.Res. 5`, sometimes 6 or 8. The rules in
force for a vote are the ones in that Congress's package plus any later amendments to it. Items
tagged **[data]** come from our Voteview cache. Items tagged **[verify]** are from general knowledge:
confirm them against clerk.house.gov, congress.gov, or the Rules Committee before you publish them.
Untagged items are well-established.

## 114th Congress (2015-2016): Speakers Boehner, then Ryan

- Rules package required CBO "dynamic scoring" of major bills.
- July 2015: Rep. Meadows filed a motion to vacate against Boehner (H.Res. 385). It was not offered
  as privileged, but it added to the pressure on Boehner, who announced his resignation in Sept.
  2015. Paul Ryan was elected Speaker in Oct. 2015.
- Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015: a debt limit suspension plus spending caps.
- Sept. 2016: **JASTA veto override** (S. 2040), the first override of an Obama veto.
- Our cache has none of this Congress. Add 114 to `MOGOV_VOTEVIEW_CONGRESSES`.

## 115th Congress (2017-2018): Speaker Ryan, unified Republican government

- Rules package: Holman rule restored (lets appropriations amendments cut specific salaries or
  programs). CUTGO kept.
- **CRA surge:** 16 disapproval resolutions enacted in 2017-18, after only one ever before [verify count].
- **Reconciliation:** AHCA passed the House in May 2017 (failed in the Senate). The Tax Cuts and Jobs
  Act was enacted in Dec. 2017. The House had to re-pass TCJA after the Senate parliamentarian
  struck provisions.
- **Shutdowns:** Jan. 20-22, 2018, and a few hours on Feb. 9, 2018. Then Dec. 22, 2018 – Jan. 25,
  2019, 35 days, which ran into the 116th.
- Conference committees were still used, for example the 2018 farm bill. They are rare afterward.

## 116th Congress (2019-2020): Speaker Pelosi, divided government

- Rules package (H.Res. 6): **PAYGO replaced CUTGO**. Gephardt rule restored. **Motion to vacate**
  limited to a resolution offered at the direction of a party caucus or conference. Select
  Committee on the Modernization of Congress created.
- Disapproval of the border national emergency was vetoed, and the **House override failed (Mar.
  2019)** [verify date]. Other vetoes of war powers and arms-sale resolutions were sustained in the
  Senate.
- Dec. 2019: first Trump impeachment (H.Res. 755).
- **May 2020: proxy voting and remote committee work** (H.Res. 965), renewed in 45-day periods. The
  CARES Act passed by voice vote (Mar. 27, 2020).
- Dec. 2020: **FY2021 NDAA veto override** in the House. The Senate finished the override on
  Jan. 1, 2021.

## 117th Congress (2021-2022): Speaker Pelosi, unified Democratic government

- Rules package (H.Res. 8): **motion to recommit loses its instructions** (no more final minority
  amendment). Proxy voting continues. Earmarks return as **Community Project Funding** (FY2022).
- Jan. 6-7, 2021: electoral count objections (Arizona, Pennsylvania), two
  `On Agreeing to the Objection` votes, both rejected **[data]**.
- Jan. 13, 2021: second Trump impeachment.
- **Reconciliation:** American Rescue Plan (Mar. 2021) and Inflation Reduction Act (Aug. 2022).
- Debt limit 2021: a one-time expedited procedure (S. 610) let the Senate raise it by majority, then
  an increase was enacted [verify].
- Dec. 2022: **Electoral Count Reform Act** (in the omnibus). Objections now need one-fifth of each
  chamber.
- 0 of 72 recorded special rules failed **[data]**.

## 118th Congress (2023-2024): Speakers McCarthy, then Johnson, narrow Republican majority

- **Jan. 2023: 15 ballots** before McCarthy was elected **[data]**. The House could not swear in
  members or adopt rules until then.
- Rules package (H.Res. 5): **motion to vacate restored to any single member**. Proxy voting ended.
  CUTGO restored. Gephardt rule dropped [verify]. 72-hour text availability emphasized.
- June 2023: Fiscal Responsibility Act (debt limit suspension plus caps).
- **Rules routinely failed:** 7 of 62 recorded special rules were defeated **[data]**, mostly by
  majority-party hard-liners. Leadership turned to **suspension** for major bills, such as
  appropriations, the FY2024 NDAA conference report (Dec. 14, 2023 **[data]**), and FISA.
- **Oct. 3, 2023: McCarthy vacated** (H.Res. 757), the first Speaker removed this way. McHenry
  served as Speaker pro tempore. Four October ballots followed (Jordan three times, then Johnson
  on Oct. 25) **[data: 19 Speaker-election rows in the 118th]**.
- Dec. 1, 2023: Santos expelled. The earlier expulsion attempt, H.Res. 773 on Nov. 1, failed
  **[data]**.
- 2024: the Mayorkas impeachment failed once, then passed [verify dates]. A May 2024 motion to vacate
  against Johnson was tabled [verify]. April 2024 foreign aid bills passed under a rule that needed
  minority votes.
- 7 veto override attempts, all failed **[data]**.
- Nov. 2024: Social Security Fairness Act reached the floor through a discharge petition [verify].

## 119th Congress (2025-2026): Speaker Johnson, unified Republican government, very narrow House majority

- Jan. 3, 2025: Johnson re-elected on the first ballot **[data: 1 Speaker-election row]**.
- Rules package: **motion to vacate needs nine majority-party cosponsors** to be privileged
  [verify the exact wording].
- **Reconciliation:** the 2025 tax-and-spending reconciliation act (signed July 4, 2025) [verify
  Pub. L. number].
- **Rescissions** under the Impoundment Control Act (July 2025) [verify].
- **Shutdown Oct. 1 – Nov. 12, 2025: 43 days, the longest** [verify]. Possible further 2026 lapses

- **Discharge petitions become routine:** five `On Motion to Discharge` votes passed, Dec. 2025 –
  June 2026 **[data]**. The Epstein Files Transparency Act went from petition to passage in Nov.
  2025 [verify].
- **War powers:** eight failed `HCONRES` removal directives (Venezuela and others) **[data]**.
- 5 of 59 recorded special rules failed **[data]**.
- Jan. 8, 2026: veto overrides on H.R. 504 and H.R. 131 failed **[data]**.

## Questions to ask about any Congress in the window

1. Who held the Speakership and what was the majority's margin? This decides whether rules can pass
   on party lines.
2. Unified or divided government? This predicts vetoes, CRA use, and reconciliation.
3. What did the rules package change? Check motion to vacate, MTR, proxy voting, PAYGO/CUTGO, and
   earmarks.
4. Were there funding lapses or debt-limit deadlines? These drive clusters of same-day rules and CRs.
